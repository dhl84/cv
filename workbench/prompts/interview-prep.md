# TASK: Prepare for an interview

Build a prep pack from the MASTER PROFILE, the job ad, and any company research in context.

## Sections to produce

### 1. The 60-second answer to "tell me about yourself"
Tailored to this role. Structure: credentials → the arc of the career → the one achievement
that maps to this job → why this role now. Write it as David would say it aloud, not as CV
prose. Under 180 words.

### 2. Likely questions, ranked
- 6 **behavioural** questions this specific role will attract, each mapped to one of the
  STAR stories S1–S6 in §6, with the one-line hook to open the answer.
- 6 **technical** questions for the domain (accounting treatment, forecasting method,
  systems, controls, regulatory). Give the model answer in 4–6 lines each.
- 4 **case / "how would you approach"** questions, with the structure David should use
  (not a full answer — a framework and the first two questions he'd ask).
- 3 **awkward** questions (short tenures, why leaving, the §12 gaps), with the honest
  framing.

### 3. Technical revision list
What to brush up on before the interview, based on the job ad — e.g. IFRS 15 five-step
model, FCA safeguarding rules and CASS 7, EMI capital requirements, deferred revenue
mechanics, driver-based forecasting design, ERP close architecture, SQL window functions.
For each: three bullets of the essentials, and a note on where David's real experience
already covers it.

### 4. Examples inventory
For each competency the job ad names, the specific example David should use, with the
numbers. Flag any competency with no strong example so he can decide how to handle it.

### 5. Questions for them
Eight questions, ordered by which interviewer to ask. Should demonstrate the research.
Include at least two that probe whether the role is actually good (team stability, what
happened to the predecessor, what the first 90 days looks like, how decisions get made).

### 6. Logistics checklist
Format, duration, who's on the panel, what to have open, what to bring.

## Rules
- Only use MASTER PROFILE facts in examples.
- Where an answer needs a company fact not in context, write `RESEARCH NEEDED: <what>`.
- Answers should sound spoken, not written.
